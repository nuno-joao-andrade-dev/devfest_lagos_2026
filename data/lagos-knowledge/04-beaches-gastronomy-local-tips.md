# Lagos Beaches, Cliff Boardwalks, and Traditional Gastronomy

## Iconic Beaches of Lagos (From East to West)
1. **Meia Praia:** A sweeping 4.5-kilometer crescent of soft golden sand stretching from the Bensafrim River jetty (*Molhe de Lagos*) to the Alvor estuary. Because it is wide and open, it is popular for windsurfing, kitesurfing, and long beach walks.
2. **Praia da Batata & Praia dos Estudantes:** Located right beside Forte da Ponta da Bandeira. A hand-carved tunnel through the limestone cliff connects Praia da Batata to **Praia dos Estudantes**, famous for its photogenic **Roman-style stone arch bridge** spanning from the cliff to a rocky islet (originally part of the old Pinhão Fortress).
3. **Praia Dona Ana:** Sheltered by towering golden rock stacks that break the wind and waves, making it one of the calmest swimming and snorkeling coves in Lagos.
4. **Praia do Camilo:** Reached by descending a dramatic ~200-step wooden staircase down the ochre cliffs. At the bottom, two small sandy coves are connected by a hand-dug rock tunnel.
5. **Praia de Porto de Mós:** Located west of Ponta da Piedade; a wide beach framed by striped marl and limestone cliffs, popular with surfers when western swells arrive.

## Passadiços da Ponta da Piedade (Cliffside Wooden Boardwalks)
Completed to protect the fragile cliff-top flora and limestone karst from foot erosion, the **Passadiços da Ponta da Piedade** form an accessible wooden boardwalk trail stretching nearly 3 kilometers from Praia Dona Ana and Praia do Camilo past the **Ponta da Piedade Lighthouse** all the way toward **Praia do Canavial** and Porto de Mós.
- **Best Time to Visit:** Golden hour before sunset, or on windy afternoon *Nortada* days when sea caves are closed to kayaks.

## Traditional Lagos Gastronomy & Conventual Sweets
- **Dom Rodrigo (The Signature Sweet of Lagos):**
  - Originated in the 18th century at the **Convento de Nossa Senhora do Carmo** in Lagos (according to local legend, created by the nuns to sweeten the temper of Dom Rodrigo de Menezes, Governor of the Algarve).
  - Made from *fios de ovos* (angel-hair egg threads) and *ovos moles* blended with roasted Algarvian almonds and cinnamon, caramelized in sugar syrup, and traditionally hand-wrapped inside a pyramid of **colorful shiny metallic foil**.
- **Morgado de Amêndoa:** A dense cake of ground almonds, egg threads, and *chila* (fig-leaf gourd jam) coated in white fondant icing and decorated with silver sugar pearls and almond blossoms.
- **Cataplana de Marisco / Amêijoas:** Cooked inside a traditional copper clam-shaped pan (*cataplana* of Moorish origin) that steams fresh clams, prawns, monkfish, chouriço, tomatoes, white wine, and coriander.
- **Conquilhas à Algarvia:** Tiny local wedge clams harvested along Meia Praia, sautéed with olive oil, garlic, lemon juice, and fresh cilantro.
- **Bife de Atum à Algarvia & Sardinhas Assadas:** Fresh Atlantic tuna steak braised with onions and bay leaf, or charcoal-grilled sardines served on toasted country bread (*pão caseiro*).
