# Maritime History of Lagos: From Ancient Lacobriga to the Age of Discoveries

## Ancient Roots: Lacobriga & Zawaia
- **Lacobriga (~2000 BC – Roman Era):** Long before Lagos became a medieval port, the Conii (Cynetes) and Celts founded the fortified settlement of **Lacobriga** on **Monte Molião**, overlooking the Bensafrim River estuary. Under Roman rule, Lacobriga was a thriving fish-salting (*garum*) and maritime trade hub. In **76 BC**, the Roman rebel general **Quintus Sertorius** famously defended Lacobriga against a siege by Metellus Pius with help from the native Lusitanians.
- **Moorish Zawaia (8th – 13th Century):** During the Islamic period of Al-Gharb, the town was known as **Zawaia** (referring to a coastal lagoon or monastery). The Moors fortified the settlement with defensive walls (*Cerca Urbana*), parts of which still stand around the old town, before King Afonso III integrated the Algarve into the Kingdom of Portugal in **1249**.

## Lagos and the 15th-Century Age of Discoveries (Descobrimentos)
In the 1400s, due to its sheltered bay and proximity to North Africa, Lagos became the operational headquarters of **Infante Dom Henrique (Prince Henry the Navigator)** and the launchpad of the Portuguese Age of Discoveries:
- **Gil Eanes & Cape Bojador (1434):** Born in Lagos, the squire and navigator **Gil Eanes** sailed from Lagos in 1434 and successfully rounded **Cape Bojador** (in modern Western Sahara), shattering medieval European myths of the "Sea of Darkness" (*Mar Tenebroso*) and opening the Atlantic route to West Africa. A famous statue of Gil Eanes by Canto da Maya stands in **Praça Gil Eanes** in the heart of Lagos.
- **The Caravel Shipyard:** Lagos was the primary port where early *barcas* and *barinel* vessels were replaced by the revolutionary **Caravela Latina**—a nimble, shallow-draft ship with triangular lateen sails capable of tacking into the wind.
- **Mercado de Escravos (1444):** Located under the arches of the *Alfândega* (Customs House) in Praça do Infante, this site houses a solemn historical museum documenting the first transatlantic slave market in Europe (1444) and its lasting historical impact.

## Historic Landmarks in the Old Town
1. **Forte da Ponta da Bandeira (Forte do Pau da Bandeira):**
   - Built between **1679 and 1690** during the War of Restoration to guard the entrance to the Bensafrim River and Praia da Batata.
   - Features a stone moat, a working **drawbridge**, a tiny chapel inside tiled entirely with 17th-century blue-and-white *azulejos*, and a rooftop terrace overlooking the harbor walls and Meia Praia.
2. **Igreja de Santo António & Museu de Lagos (Dr. José Formosinho):**
   - Built in 1707 and reconstructed after the 1755 earthquake, its plain whitewashed exterior hides one of the most spectacular interiors of **Baroque gilded woodcarving (*talha dourada*)** in Portugal, populated by cherubs, grape vines, and trompe-l'oeil ceiling paintings.
   - **The Military Saint Legend:** In 1691, the statue of **Saint Anthony** in this church was officially enlisted in the Lagos Artillery Regiment (#2), promoted to Captain-General by King Dom Pedro II, and received a regular military salary for centuries to fund the church's maintenance.
3. **Castelo dos Governadores & Porta de São Gonçalo:**
   - The medieval castle facade flanked by twin towers where the Governors of the Algarve resided, right beside the arched **Porta de São Gonçalo** gateway facing the sea, which holds a small shrine honoring **São Gonçalo de Lagos** (born in Lagos in 1360).
